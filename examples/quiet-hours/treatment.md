# Quiet Hours

Fictional campaign for **HUSH / Desk Mat**. The audience is people who share a room while working at home. The problem is a desk that sounds like a demolition site when keys land on it. The ad exaggerates the roommate's reaction and leaves acoustic performance unquantified.

## Three concepts

1. **Eviction notice.** A miniature clipboard marks a keyring as a noisy tenant. The same keyring lands in an illustrated felt rectangle. The clipboard stamps it “ALLOWED.” Select this for the immediate accusation, visual reversal, and readable product silhouette.
2. **Silent cinema.** A melodramatic title card tries to communicate the noise in a silent film. Pass because the conceit depends on reading several cards.
3. **Workplace hearing.** Illustrated desk objects testify against the keyring. Pass because setup and cast compete with the product in 15 seconds.

## Treatment

Open on **EVICT THE KEYS?** over a coral citation card with a keyring already visible. The room is reduced to an overhead ink-and-paper diagram. A key lands. The citation clipboard slides into frame, then the shape and material of the HUSH mat claim the desk. A second key drop gets a comic approval stamp. Finish on **See the desk mat** at 12 seconds. The CTA is a fictional showroom invitation. No pricing, objective acoustic claim, or sale terms.

The alternative **WHO CALLED SECURITY?** changes the opening accusation only. Both share the same product appearance, animation, rights, and ending.

Use an original illustrated packshot with a cobalt base, warm ivory HU monogram, and coral ink. Shoot no real faces. Use an original local motion clip for the key drop handoff. Score is an original minimal desk percussion pattern with a short silence before the stamp. Compare hook comprehension with a sound-off viewing and use the three-second hold rate as a future metric.
