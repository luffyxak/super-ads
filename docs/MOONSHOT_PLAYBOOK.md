# Moonshot Playbook — Challenger Ads, Tanmay Bhatt School

Moonshot is Tanmay Bhatt's company. Its ads are the benchmark for challenger,
Gen-Z, Indian advertising in the 2020s. This playbook is inspired by that school.
It is not a copy of any Moonshot ad. Write your own ads with these patterns.

## Who Moonshot is
- Founder: Tanmay Bhatt, comedian and creator. Company: Moonshot.
- Known for: CRED ads (Rahul Dravid as "Indiranagar Ka Gunda", Jim Sarbh, and the
  series of celebrity-subversion films), and a run of fintech / startup ads that
  made boring categories funny.
- Why it works: the ads treat the audience as smart, the celebrity as a joke,
  and the product as a simple truth.

## 5 Moonshot patterns

### 1. Celebrity subversion, not endorsement
Cast a star against type. Rahul Dravid — "The Wall", calm, patient — is angry,
shouting in traffic. That is the joke. The star is not selling. The star is breaking.
Rule: if the star could be swapped for any star and the ad still works, it is endorsement.
If only that star, in that role, makes it funny, it is subversion.

### 2. Deadpan anger
A serious person, angrily explaining a simple thing. The anger is the hook.
The calm after the product is the payoff. Deadpan beats shouting.
Rule: the actor is angry at the process, not at the viewer. The viewer is on the actor's side.

### 3. The form is the villain
The enemy is not a competitor. The enemy is the form, the queue, the 10-step process,
the boring bank, the confusing plan. Punch up at the process.
Rule: name the villain in one line. "10 pages for one payment." That is the enemy.

### 4. Medium is the joke
The ad knows it is an ad. It parodies the category, the format, the trend.
It does not pretend to be a film. It is a joke about films, about ads, about the product.
Rule: if the ad could run as a straight TVC with no irony, it is not Moonshot. Add the irony.

### 5. Demo is the proof
Do not claim it. Show it, timed, on screen. Two taps. Four seconds. Done.
The demo is the proof. The claim is the demo.
Rule: if you cannot show it in the ad, do not claim it in the ad.

## How to write a Moonshot-style ad
1. Strategy: one punch, 5 words. Proof: a timed demo. Enemy: the process.
2. Concept: one star, cast against type, one anger, one calm.
3. Script: hook in 2 seconds, anger in the setup, demo in the twist, calm in the payoff, CTA last.
4. Performance: deadpan. No shouting at the camera. No winking at the audience.
5. Edit: fast cuts in the anger, slow hold in the calm. The contrast is the joke.

## Moonshot-style checklist
- [ ] Star is cast against type — name the type, name the break
- [ ] Enemy is the process, not a person
- [ ] Demo is timed on screen, not claimed in words
- [ ] First line is a hook, not a greeting
- [ ] No jargon. Hinglish where natural.
- [ ] CTA is last 3 seconds only, one action

## What Moonshot is NOT
- Not a celebrity endorsement. Not a star reading a script.
- Not a trend chase. Not a meme for its own sake.
- Not a hard sell. Not a 30-second product explanation.
- Not a copy. Do not copy a Moonshot ad shot for shot. Learn the pattern, write your own.

## Other challenger agencies to study
Study them the same way: name the pattern, do not copy the ad.
- Indian challenger agencies and collectives making Gen-Z, meme-aware, celebrity-subversion ads.
- Global challenger shops: Wieden+Kennedy (Nike, Old Spice), Droga5, Mother, Uncommon.
- Creator-led studios: people who came from YouTube / stand-up / short video, not only from TV.
- For each, write 5 lines: hook, punch, twist, meme frame, CTA. That is how you learn.

## When NOT to use Moonshot
- A premium jewellery ad that needs warmth, not anger. Use Diwali patterns instead.
- A religious / festive moment that needs respect, not irony.
- A children's product. Deadpan anger does not fit.
- A brand that cannot take a joke about itself. Moonshot needs a brave client.
