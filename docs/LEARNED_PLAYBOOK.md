# Learned Playbook — Patterns from 150+ Ads Studied (2010–2026)

This playbook is the taste layer. It is what 150+ booming ads have in common,
across India, USA, UK, Europe, Japan, Brazil, Australia and Thailand.
Use it before you write a concept. If your concept breaks a pattern, know why.

## How to use this playbook
- Pick ONE pattern per ad. Do not stack three patterns.
- Name the pattern in the concept board. If you cannot name it, the concept is vague.
- Patterns are tools, not rules. Break one deliberately, not accidentally.

## 10 timeless patterns

### 1. Hook in 1–2 seconds
The first frame is the ad. Dove Sketches, Old Spice, Google Reunion — all land in 2 seconds.
No logo card first. No greeting. No slow open.
Test: watch the first 2 seconds sound-off. If you do not know what is happening, fix it.

### 2. One punch per ad
One idea, one joke, one feeling, one CTA. Snickers: "You're not you when you're hungry."
One line. One word — "hangry" — entered the language.
Test: can a stranger repeat the punch in 5 words after one watch? If not, cut.

### 3. Emotion before product
The product appears late, clearly, once. Google Reunion is a short film about two friends.
The product is the tool that reunites them, not the hero. Ariel Share the Load asks
whose job laundry is — the product is the answer, not the question.
Test: remove the product. Is there still a story? If yes, the product earns its place back.

### 4. Celebrity subversion, not endorsement
A star cast against type. CRED's Rahul Dravid — "The Wall" — angry. That is the joke.
A celebrity reading a script is an endorsement. A celebrity breaking type is a story.
Test: would the ad still work if the star was unknown? If no, the star is carrying it.

### 5. Medium is the joke
The format is part of the idea. Volkswagen The Force was released on YouTube 4 days
before the Super Bowl — it invented the pre-release teaser. Tide's "It's a Tide Ad"
parodied every Super Bowl cliché in one night. The medium is not a container. It is the joke.
Test: could this ad run in any format, unchanged? If yes, the medium is not being used.

### 6. Challenger energy
Punch up. Small brand, big idea, honest voice. Samsung mocked iPhone queues.
CRED mocked boring bank ads. A challenger does not pretend to be the leader.
It says what the leader cannot say.
Test: who is the enemy in this ad? If there is no enemy, there is no challenger energy.

### 7. Meme-able frame
One frame, one line, one sound that can be screenshotted and shared.
Volvo Epic Split: Van Damme between two trucks. That frame is the ad.
Budweiser Puppy Love: puppy + Clydesdale. That frame is the ad.
Test: screenshot the ad at a random second. Is there a frame you would share? Name it.

### 8. No hard sell in first 80%
Earn the last 3 seconds. The CTA is the payoff, not the opening.
Dove, Always #LikeAGirl, Nike Dream Crazy — all earn the CTA by telling a story first.
Test: move the CTA to second 5. Does the ad collapse? If yes, the story was not earning it.

### 9. Sound-off works, sound-on is better
Half the audience watches sound-off. Captions on, always. Visual storytelling first.
A silent film test: watch the ad with no sound. Do you still get it?
Test: watch sound-off on a phone. If you are lost, fix the visuals, not the captions.

### 10. Rewatchable
A second watch reveals a joke you missed. Jeep Groundhog Day rewards a second watch.
Tide's meta-ad rewards a second watch. A one-watch ad is a one-watch ad.
Test: watch it twice. Did the second watch add anything? If not, add a layer.

## 5 Moonshot patterns
See `MOONSHOT_PLAYBOOK.md` for the full version. Short version:
1. Deadpan anger: a serious person, angrily explaining a simple thing.
2. The form is the villain: the process, not the person, is the enemy.
3. The star is the joke: cast against type, let the type break.
4. No jargon: say it the way a person would say it, in Hinglish where that is natural.
5. The demo is the proof: show it timed on screen, do not claim it in words.

## Diwali patterns
See `DIWALI_PLAYBOOK.md`. Short version:
- Homecoming: the house lights up when the person returns.
- Gifting as peace: a box ends an argument.
- Local shop: the ad is about the shopkeeper, the brand is the guest.
- Light in a dark moment: a diya, a phone call home, a small kindness.
- Family argument resolved: the tension is real, the resolution is warm.

## Country patterns
See `COUNTRY_PLAYBOOK.md`. Short version:
- India: family, festival, Hinglish, Tier-2/3 clarity, value + emotion together.
- USA: Super Bowl scale, celebrity, humour, challenger, one big idea.
- UK / Europe: understatement, wit, craft, purpose without preaching.
- Japan: detail, ritual, quiet, product as craft.
- Brazil: music, colour, joy, community.
- Australia: dry humour, outdoors, straight talk.
- Thailand: emotional storytelling, humour, family, a twist at the end.

## What kills ads
- Three messages in one ad.
- A hook that is a question with no tension.
- A celebrity reading a script.
- A product shot that looks like a catalogue.
- A CTA in the middle.
- A trend used as the whole idea, with no story underneath.
- A claim with no proof. A person with no consent. A licence with no file.

## How to learn from an ad
For every ad you study, write 5 lines:
1. Hook: what happens in second 1–2?
2. Punch: what is the one idea, in 5 words?
3. Twist: what changes by second 5?
4. Meme frame: what gets screenshotted?
5. CTA: what, where, by when — and is it only in the last 3 seconds?
Do this for 10 ads. You will see the patterns yourself. That is the point of this repo:
learn the patterns, then make your own ads with them — do not copy the ads.
