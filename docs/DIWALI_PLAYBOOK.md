# Diwali Playbook — Festive Ads 2010–2026

Built from 53 Diwali ads studied, 2010–2026, plus Dhanteras, Durga Puja and
festive-season work. Use one pattern per ad. The ritual is the story.

## The 5 Diwali patterns

### 1. Homecoming
A person returns. The house lights up. Gold, sweets or a gift is the welcome, not the point.
Example shape: a son returns late, the mother has kept a diya lit, the family eats together.
Rule: the return is the story. The product is the welcome.

### 2. Gifting as peace
An argument ends with a gift. Neighbours argue about parking, walls, pets, cricket —
a box of sweets ends it. The gift is the peace offering.
Rule: the argument must be real and small. The gift must be the resolution, not a bribe.

### 3. Local shop
The ad is about the shopkeeper, the lane, the neighbourhood. The brand is the guest.
Example shape: a pin-code ad where the local store is the hero, not the brand.
Rule: name the shop, the lane, the city. Specific beats generic.

### 4. Light in a dark moment
A diya in a dark lane. A phone call home. A delivery partner invited in for sweets.
Small light, big feeling. No fireworks needed.
Rule: one light, one kindness, one moment. Do not add spectacle on top.

### 5. Family argument resolved
A family argues — about money, about gold, about who hosts Diwali — and resolves it warmly.
The tension is real. The resolution is warm, not preachy.
Rule: the argument is about something the audience recognises. The resolution is not a lecture.

## Diwali ad checklist
- [ ] One pattern named, from the 5 above
- [ ] Ritual named: what is actually happening? (diya, gift, first gold, sweets shared)
- [ ] Family / tension named in one line each
- [ ] Hinglish natural, not translated
- [ ] Offer: what, how much, till when, where — with proof
- [ ] Launch date: festival date + launch date + shoot date (start 6 weeks before)
- [ ] Tier-2/3 clarity: a family in Indore or Lucknow sees themselves in it

## What works in Diwali ads
- Warm light: diya, practicals, golden hour. Not cold studio light.
- Hands: hands exchanging a box, lighting a diya, holding bangles. Hands are the story.
- Sound: soft music, diya crackle, family laughter. Silence can be the most festive sound.
- Gold: show it in use, on a wrist, in a hand — not on a pedestal.
- Small moments: a box of sweets, a phone call, a nod from nani. Small beats spectacle.

## What kills Diwali ads
- Diyas + fireworks + "Happy Diwali" and nothing else. That is a card, not an ad.
- A metro family pretending to be "all of India".
- A festive discount with no end date and no proof.
- A religious moment used as a backdrop with no respect.
- Launching after Diwali. Plan 6 weeks ahead.

## Dhanteras notes
- Dhanteras is the buying day. Gold, silver, utensils. The ad should land before Dhanteras,
  not on Diwali day. Offer dates matter: Dhanteras date, Diwali date, offer end date.
- Hallmark / BIS: show it. Gold claims need proof. Making charges need a real number.

## Durga Puja notes
- Puja is about community, art, and homecoming to Kolkata and Bengal.
- The most remembered Puja ads are about people making the idol, not about buying.
- Respect the ritual. The idol, the pandal, the dhak — show them with care.

## Language
- Hinglish where the audience speaks Hinglish. Do not force it.
- One line in Hindi can carry more feeling than a paragraph in English.
- Captions for every line. Sound-off must still work.
