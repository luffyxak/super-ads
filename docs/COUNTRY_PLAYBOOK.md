# Country Playbook — Best Ads by Country, Last 20 Years (2010–2026)

Built from 70 top famous ads studied, country-wise. Use the country pattern as a
starting point, not a stereotype. Every country has range. Learn the pattern, then break it well.

## India
- Pattern: family, festival, Hinglish, Tier-2/3 clarity, value + emotion together.
- What works: homecoming, gifting as peace, local shop, a nod from an elder, a small kindness.
- What kills it: metro stereotype, translated English, spectacle with no story.
- Key ads to study: Google Reunion (2013), CRED Rahul Dravid (2021), Cadbury Kuch Khaas Hai,
  Fevicol, Surf Excel Daag Achhe Hain, Ariel Share the Load, Jio launch (2016).
- Language: Hinglish, natural. Captions on. Family in the frame.

## USA
- Pattern: Super Bowl scale, celebrity, humour, challenger, one big idea.
- What works: a star used right, a story that earns 30 seconds, a meme frame.
- What kills it: a star reading a script, a joke with no product, a 30s ad that could be 15s.
- Key ads to study: Old Spice (2010), Volkswagen The Force (2011), Budweiser Puppy Love (2014),
  Snickers Betty White (2010), Jeep Groundhog Day (2020), Nike Dream Crazy (2018).
- Language: direct, confident, one punch. No hedging.

## UK
- Pattern: understatement, wit, craft, purpose without preaching.
- What works: a quiet idea, well made. Humour that is dry, not loud.
- What kills it: shouting, over-explaining, a purpose ad that lectures.
- Key ads to study: John Lewis Christmas films, Guinness, Honda "Cog" / "Hands" school.
- Language: understatement. Let the audience finish the joke.

## Europe (France, Germany, Spain, Nordics)
- Pattern: craft, design, cinema, purpose with elegance.
- What works: visual storytelling, music, a filmic look. Volvo Epic Split is Swedish craft.
- What kills it: a global ad dubbed badly, a joke that does not travel.
- Key ads to study: Volvo Epic Split (2013), Orange WoMen's Football (2024, France),
  Evian Rollerbabies (2009, France).
- Language: visual first. The idea must work without words.

## Japan
- Pattern: detail, ritual, quiet, product as craft.
- What works: a small moment, perfectly made. Respect for the object.
- What kills it: loudness for its own sake, a joke that needs explaining.
- Key ads to study: Japanese craft / ritual ads, minimal product films.
- Language: quiet, precise. Let the detail carry it.

## Brazil
- Pattern: music, colour, joy, community.
- What works: rhythm, dance, a crowd, a celebration. The ad feels like a party.
- What kills it: a muted, grey ad that could be from anywhere.
- Key ads to study: Brazilian carnival / football / music-led ads.
- Language: music first. The sound is the hook.

## Australia
- Pattern: dry humour, outdoors, straight talk.
- What works: a laconic joke, a real place, no pretension.
- What kills it: pretension, over-polish, a joke that tries too hard.
- Key ads to study: Metro Trains "Dumb Ways to Die" (2012, Melbourne) — a PSA as a pop song.
- Language: straight, dry, short sentences.

## Thailand
- Pattern: emotional storytelling, humour, family, a twist at the end.
- What works: a long story with a twist, a tear and a laugh in the same ad.
- What kills it: a short ad with no story, a twist with no setup.
- Key ads to study: Thai life-insurance and telecom story ads.
- Language: story first. The twist must be earned by the setup.

## How to use this playbook
1. Name the country and the pattern in the concept board.
2. Write the ad in that country's voice — then check it would still work in India, if that is the market.
3. For a global ad, pick one pattern and make it visual. Visual travels. Wordplay often does not.
4. For an India ad, do not copy a USA ad shot for shot. Learn the punch, rewrite the story in India.
