# Ad Strategy

## When to use
- Starting a new ad, campaign, or Diwali / festive push.
- The brief is vague, or the product has no clear reason to care.
- Two concepts both look good and you need a test to pick one.

## Workflow
1. Read the brief in `templates/brief-template.md`. Fill the 5 blanks: audience, problem, product truth, proof, CTA.
2. Name the audience in one line: who, where, what they already believe.
   India: name the city tier, language mix, and festival context if any.
3. Name the one punch: the single idea the viewer will remember in 5 words.
   If you need two sentences, it is not one punch. Cut it.
4. Name the proof: one fact, demo, price, or story that makes the punch believable.
   No proof, no claim. Write the source next to the proof.
5. Name the CTA in the last 3 seconds only: what, where, by when.
6. Name the enemy: what are we against? Boring ads, high prices, slow apps, confusing plans.
   Challenger energy needs an enemy.
7. Write the strategy in one page. Test it: can a stranger repeat the punch after one watch?
8. Hand off to the creative director with the strategy page only. No scripts yet.

## Output format
- Audience: one line
- Problem: one line
- One punch: 5 words max
- Proof: one fact + source
- CTA: one line, last 3 seconds
- Enemy: one line
- What we will NOT say: 3 lines

## Quality bar
- A 12-year-old in Tier-2 India can repeat the punch.
- The punch is about the viewer, not the brand.
- The CTA is specific: app, store, link, date, price.
- No jargon. No "revolutionary", "best-in-class", "seamless" without proof.

## Examples
- Jewellery Diwali: Audience — families buying first gold for Diwali in Jaipur.
  Punch — "Gold that comes home for Diwali." Proof — BIS hallmark, making charges 12% off till 8 Nov.
- Fintech: Audience — 22-year-olds who hate bank forms. Punch — "UPI in 2 taps."
  Proof — live demo in the ad, timed on screen.
- Product launch: Audience — gym-goers who hate sugary drinks. Punch — "Mango, no sugar crash."
  Proof — 0g added sugar on the label, shown close-up.

## Anti-patterns
- Three messages in one ad. Pick one.
- "For everyone" as an audience. That is no audience.
- A punch that could fit any brand. Make it ownable.
