# Ad Diwali / Festive

## When to use
- Diwali, Dhanteras, Durga Puja, Eid, Christmas, or a wedding / festive season push.
- The brief says "festive" and the first idea is "diyas and fireworks". Go deeper.
- The ad must work in Tier-2/3 India, in Hinglish, for a family, not only for a metro buyer.

## Workflow
1. Read `docs/DIWALI_PLAYBOOK.md`. Pick one festive pattern, not three:
   homecoming, gifting as peace, local shop, light in a dark moment, family argument resolved.
2. Name the ritual in the ad: what is actually happening? Diya lighting, gift exchange,
   first gold purchase, sweets shared with a delivery partner, neighbours making up.
   The ritual is the story. The product is the guest, not the host.
3. Write the family in one line: who is there, what do they want, what is the tension?
   A festive ad without tension is a greeting card, not an ad.
4. Use Hinglish naturally. Do not translate an English ad word for word.
   Say it the way a family in Indore or Lucknow would say it.
5. Show the local shop, the neighbourhood, the small moment. Big spectacle is optional.
   The most remembered Diwali ads are small: a box of sweets, a diya, a phone call home.
6. Handle gold / price honestly: hallmark, making charges, offer dates, store name.
   Festive offers must have a real end date. No fake urgency.
7. Check the calendar: Dhanteras, Diwali, Bhai Dooj, Puja dates. Do not launch a Diwali ad
   after Diwali. Plan the shoot 6 weeks before the festival.
8. Hand off with the festive pattern named, the ritual named, and the offer dates checked.

## Output format
- Festive pattern: name from the playbook + why this brand
- Ritual: one line — what is actually happening?
- Family / tension: one line each
- Offer: what, how much, till when, where — with proof
- Language: Hinglish lines as spoken, not translated
- Launch date: festival date + launch date + shoot date

## Quality bar
- A family in Tier-2 India sees themselves in it, not a metro stereotype.
- The product could be removed and the story would still be a story — then the product
  earns its place back in the last 3 seconds.
- The offer is true, dated, and in the brief. No "up to" without the real number.
- The ad works without fireworks. Light, warmth and people carry it.

## Examples
- Homecoming: a son returns, the house lights up, gold is the welcome, not the point.
- Gifting as peace: neighbours argue about parking, a box of sweets ends it.
- Local shop: the ad is about the shopkeeper, the brand is the guest.
- Light: a diya in a dark lane, a delivery partner is invited in for sweets.

## Anti-patterns
- Diyas + fireworks + "Happy Diwali" and nothing else. That is a card, not an ad.
- A metro family pretending to be "all of India".
- A festive discount with no end date and no proof.
- A religious moment used as a backdrop with no respect for the ritual.
