# Festive patterns — reference for ad-diwali-festive

## Five patterns (from 53 Diwali ads, 2010-2026)
1. Homecoming: someone returns, the house lights up, gold is the welcome.
2. Gifting as peace: a small argument ends with a box of sweets.
3. Local shop: the ad is about the shopkeeper; the brand is the guest.
4. Light in a dark moment: a diya in a dark lane; a stranger is invited in.
5. Family argument resolved: tension, then a gift or a call home ends it.

## Rules
- Name the ritual: diya lighting, gift exchange, first gold purchase, sweets
  shared with a delivery partner, neighbours making up. The ritual is the
  story; the product is the guest, not the host.
- A festive ad without tension is a greeting card, not an ad.
- Hinglish as a family in Indore or Lucknow would speak it. Never translate
  an English ad word for word.
- Gold and price honestly: hallmark, making charges, offer dates, store name.
  Festive offers must have a real end date. No fake urgency.
- Check the calendar: Dhanteras, Diwali, Bhai Dooj, Puja dates. Do not launch
  a Diwali ad after Diwali. Plan the shoot six weeks before the festival.
